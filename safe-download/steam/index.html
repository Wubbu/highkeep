Download Steam safely

Steam is Valve's game store and launcher. Fake download sites for apps this popular are a common way malware spreads, so here's exactly where to get the real one.

The quickest safe way: winget

Windows 10 and 11 come with winget, Microsoft's package manager. It downloads Steam from the source listed in Microsoft's catalog, so there's no search result to get wrong. Open Terminal (right-click the Start button) and paste:

winget install --id Valve.Steam -e

Or go to store.steampowered.com by typing the address yourself, rather than clicking a search ad.

Watch out for

  • "Vote for my team" and "free skins" links lead to fake Steam login pages that steal accounts and inventories.
  • Steam never asks you to log in through a pop-up on another site to trade or claim items.
  • Free in-game currency, skins or items in exchange for a download or your login are always scams.
  • Cheats, mod menus and "executors" are the most common way gaming PCs get password stealers. They often ask you to turn your antivirus off, which is the tell.
  • Search ads above the real results. Scammers buy ads for popular app names, so the first link can be a fake. Scroll past the ads, or type the address yourself.
  • Look-alike addresses: an extra word, a dash, a swapped letter or a different ending (.org instead of .com).
  • Downloads that arrive as a password-protected .zip, or as a .scr, .bat, .js or .vbs file. Real installers are .exe or .msi files.
  • Pages that tell you to turn off your antivirus or Windows Defender before installing.

Check the file before you run it

  1. The download should come from cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com (your browser's download list shows where a file came from).
  2. Right-click the installer → Properties → Digital Signatures. It should be signed by the company or project behind Steam.
  3. If Windows SmartScreen or your antivirus warns you, stop and check where the file came from before clicking past it.
