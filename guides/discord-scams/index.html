Common Discord Scams in 2026 and How to Avoid Them

Updated November 2026

Discord scams mostly arrive from people you trust, because the accounts sending them were stolen first. Here are the ones that come up again and again.

Free Nitro links

A message says you've been gifted Nitro, with a link to a site whose name is almost Discord (an l instead of an i, an extra word). It's a copy of Discord's login page. Real Nitro gifts use discord.gift links and show up inside Discord as a gift card with an Accept button. They never ask you to log in.

"I accidentally reported you"

Someone says they reported you by mistake and you need to talk to a "Discord staff member" to avoid a ban. The "staff member" asks for your login, a code, or a payment. Discord staff don't contact people through DMs this way.

"Try my new game"

A friend asks you to test a game they made and sends a download. It's usually a password stealer, and their account was taken the same way. Check with them another way first, like a call or a text.

QR code logins and console pastes

Never scan a Discord QR code someone sends you to "verify" or "get a role": it logs them in as you. And never paste anything into Discord's developer console, whoever asks.

Protect your account

  • Turn on two-factor authentication (User Settings → My Account).
  • Use a password you don't use anywhere else.
  • If your account sends scam messages, change your password straight away. That signs out other sessions.
