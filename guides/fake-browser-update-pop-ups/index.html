Fake Browser Update Pop-ups: Chrome, Edge and Firefox Never Ask Like This

Updated November 2026

You're reading a normal-looking website and the page turns into a warning: "Your Chrome is out of date. Update now." It has the right logo and a download button. It's fake, every time.

Why it's always fake

Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Brave and Opera update themselves in the background. When they need a restart, they tell you inside the browser's own menu or toolbar, never inside a web page. A website can't know whether your browser is up to date, and it has no business offering you a browser installer.

These pages are usually hacked websites that have been made to show a fake update screen. The "update" is often a password stealer or a tool that gives attackers remote control.

How to update for real

  • Chrome: menu (three dots) → Help → About Google Chrome.
  • Edge: menu (three dots) → Help and feedback → About Microsoft Edge.
  • Firefox: menu (three lines) → Help → About Firefox.

Each one checks for updates and installs them right there.

If you already downloaded the "update"

Don't open it, and delete it. If you already ran it, disconnect from the internet, run a full antivirus scan and change your passwords from another device, starting with your email.
