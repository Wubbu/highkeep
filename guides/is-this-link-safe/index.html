Is This Link Safe? How to Check Before You Click

Updated November 2026

A link can say anything; what matters is where it actually goes. Here's how to check in a few seconds.

1. See the real address

On a computer, hover over the link and look at the bottom-left corner of the browser. On a phone, press and hold the link to see the address without opening it.

2. Read the domain from the right

The real owner of an address is the part just before the first single slash. In paypal.com.account-check.example/login, the site is account-check.example, not PayPal. Read it from right to left: the ending, then the name right before it.

3. Watch for look-alikes

Swapped letters (rn for m, l for i), extra words (-login, -secure, -gift), and unusual endings like .gift, .top or .xyz on a brand you know.

4. Be suspicious of pressure

"Your account will be closed", "you've won", "only today": urgency is the scammer's main tool. Go to the site yourself by typing its address or using its app.
