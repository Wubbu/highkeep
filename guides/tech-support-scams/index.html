Tech Support Scams: When Someone Wants to Connect to Your PC

Updated November 2026

Tech support scams start one of two ways: a pop-up that fills your screen and says your computer is infected (often with a loud alarm), or a phone call from someone claiming to be Microsoft, your bank, or your antivirus company.

How it goes

  1. They tell you to call a number or install a remote access app like AnyDesk or TeamViewer.
  2. Once connected, they show you "proof" of infection: normal Windows logs or error lists that every PC has.
  3. They charge for a fix, ask you to log into your bank "to check for fraud", or install their own software to come back later.

The rules that stop it

  • Microsoft, banks and antivirus companies don't put phone numbers in browser pop-ups and don't call you about viruses.
  • Never let anyone who contacted you first connect to your PC.
  • A pop-up that won't close isn't a real virus warning. Close the browser with Ctrl+Shift+Esc → Task Manager → End task.

If you already let them in

Disconnect from the internet, uninstall the remote access app they asked for, run a full antivirus scan, and call your bank using the number on your card. Change your important passwords from another device.
